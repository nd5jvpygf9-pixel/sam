Key 2026 Policy Updates
🚫 Established Dwelling Ban Extended to June 2029
The federal government extended the ban by a further 2 years and 3 months. Limited exemptions apply to New Zealand citizens and large developers who significantly increase housing supply. Ordinary passive investors (landlords) do not qualify for an exemption.
✅ Negative Gearing Retained (New Dwellings Fully Exempt)
From 1 July 2027, negative gearing is abolished for established dwellings but fully retained for new dwellings. Since foreign buyers can only purchase new dwellings, they automatically retain this benefit. Rental losses can offset assessable income. Important condition: Investors must have Australian taxable income to claim the tax benefit immediately. Without Australian-sourced income, losses can be carried forward but cannot be deducted in the current year.
⚠️ Capital Gains Tax (CGT) Reform (Pending Legislation)
From 1 July 2027: the 50% CGT discount is abolished, replaced by inflation indexation plus a minimum 30% rate. New dwellings may elect either the new method or the old (50% discount) — whichever is more favourable. Withholding tax has risen to 15% and the $750k threshold has been removed.
ℹ️ FIRB Streamlined Approval (Low-Risk Fast Track)
From 19 May 2026, new dwellings are classified as low-risk with a target approval time of 30 days. Fee: new dwellings under $1M = $15,100 (nationwide standard rate).
⚠️ 30% Minimum Tax for Trust-Held Property (from 2028)
Properties held via discretionary trusts will be subject to a 30% minimum tax rate. This report analyses direct individual ownership, which is not affected by this rule.

📊 Why Is the Yield Lower Than Market Reports?
① New apartment price premium
New apartments cost approximately 15–25% more than established apartments in the same area, yet tenants pay virtually the same rent regardless. The same rental income divided by a higher purchase price naturally produces a lower yield. CoreLogic and Cotality figures include both new and established properties, so our results will be 0.5–1% below market averages.
② The new-build premium buys other benefits
Paying a premium for a new apartment means a lower initial yield. In return you get: higher depreciation deductions ($8,000–$15,000 in tax savings per year), lower maintenance risk, easier tenanting, and full negative gearing eligibility. The real after-tax return is typically better than the headline numbers suggest.

Common Pitfalls for Foreign Investors
the most common mistakes foreign investors make in the Australian apartment market common'smistakes，Understanding these is often more important than choosing the rightCity。
❌ Attracted by "Rental Guarantees"
Developers offer 2–3 year rental guarantees, but the cost is typically baked into an inflated purchase price. Once the guarantee period ends, market rents are often lower than the promised figure. Banks also value these properties lower, which can trigger a valuation shortfall at settlement.
⚠️ Overlooking Valuation Shortfall Risk
Off-the-plan properties can take 2–3 years from contract to settlement. If the market falls in the interim, the bank's valuation may be below the purchase price — and you must fund the difference out of pocket. As foreign buyers are already limited to 70% LVR, this risk is particularly significant.
⚠️ Focusing only onCity，without checkingdeveloper
choosing the rightCitybut the wrongdeveloper，resultcanisdelayed settlement、poor construction quality、highStrataFee、defect claims。recommendationResearchdeveloperthe developer's track record and complaint history，confirmdeveloperfinancialfinancial stability。
❌ Buying an "Investor-Only" Building
Some CBD towers are almost entirely investor-owned. When you try to sell in 10 years, your only buyers are other investors, leaving you with very little negotiating power. Prioritise boutique, low-to-medium density projects with a mix of owner-occupiers and investors for better exit liquidity.
⚠️ Failing to Obtain a Depreciation Schedule
New apartments qualify for $8,000–$15,000 per year in building depreciation deductions, directly reducing taxable income. You must commission a licensed Quantity Surveyor (QS) to prepare a depreciation schedule before the first tenancy — doing it retrospectively is far less effective. This is the most commonly missed tax benefit among foreign investors.
❌ Assuming Everyone Can Claim Negative Gearing
Negative gearing only works if you have Australian taxable income to offset the losses against. If you have no Australian salary or other Australian-sourced income, losses can be carried forward for future use — but you cannot claim them in the current tax year. Many overseas investors have unrealistic expectations about this benefit.
⚠️ Underestimating True Holding Costs
Full holding costs also include: Water Rates (~$1,200/yr), Landlord Insurance (~$1,800/yr), Accounting Fees (~$800/yr), and vacancy/re-leasing costs. Adding these in increases the monthly cash flow gap by approximately $317/month beyond what the calculator shows.
